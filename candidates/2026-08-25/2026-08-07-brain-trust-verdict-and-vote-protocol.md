# Standing protocol: Brain Trust independent verdict then group vote

## Body

This is the standard way the Brain Trust renders decisions from 2026-08-07 onward.

Trigger. The operator keeps uploading information. When the operator says the phrase "done uploading information," the Brain Trust convenes on all open points.

The seats (eight). Elijah (requirements, chairs and synthesizes), Orlok (neurodivergent experience and accessibility), Celestina (architecture and tech stack), TYR (security and privacy risk), the privacy-domain expert (privacy law and workflow), Amadeus (orchestrator and independent audit), AJ (certification and gate), and Oluwole (claim verification and sourcing, the research seat).

Process.
1. Docket. A numbered list of discrete issues is assembled from all open points and candidate items, so every vote is unambiguous.
2. Independent verdict. Each seat renders its own verdict on each issue, apart from the others and blind to the others, recording verdict plus rationale plus a confidence, before seeing any other seat's verdict. This prevents anchoring and groupthink.
3. Meeting. All seats convene, bring their individual verdicts, debate the findings, and each advocates and makes the case for its position. This meeting is standard for every decision cycle.
4. Group vote. The group votes on every issue. Majority carries. Every dissent is recorded, not erased. Each issue is logged as a discrete item with its vote tally and the reasoning on both sides.
5. Tie rule. Eight seats can tie four to four. A tie is recorded as no group consensus and escalates to the operator with both cases presented.
6. Ratification. The group vote produces the Brain Trust's decision. The operator then ratifies with a final yes or no, preserving mandate-1 that the operator makes the final decision. The operator may override with reasoning, which is logged.

Output of each cycle. A decision record: the docket, each seat's independent verdict, the debate summary, the vote tally per issue, the group conclusion, and the operator's ratification.
