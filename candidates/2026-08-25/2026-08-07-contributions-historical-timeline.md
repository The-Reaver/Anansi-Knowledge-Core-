# Contributions and Historical Timeline

## Body

Pinned Anansi note. Factual record of how the project and its method developed, kept current as the source for the "How We Got Here" brief. Not written to glorify. It records the operator's driving contributions: the questions, the research, the turn in method, and the decision to bring in the architect.

## Stage one: building GEO Suite with discipline
Built GEO Suite as a working demo, not slides. Rule from the outset: plan first, build in slices, verify each slice before the next. Same rule applied to the Site Generator, which builds in ordered verified layers. Contribution: set the build discipline.

## Stage two: naming the real asset
Shifted focus from the app to what powers it. Called the Knowledge Core the real asset, because report and site depth is a function of validated material held. Requested a plain-language explainer, a rigorous maintenance plan so nothing untrue stays live, and a lawyer as a permanent in-loop validator. Contribution: identified the compounding asset and demanded an upkeep and validation method.

## Stage three: the intelligence funnel and the research
Asked whether the intelligence-gathering structure should be reused from GEO Suite, and researched what a real practitioner needs, benchmarked against how large firms handle the work. Ran deep research across more than one system in parallel, fed back in rounds of clarifying questions, and pushed decisions to the group for review before locking. Contribution: ran a real multi-source research process, not a single query.

## Stage four: the turn, we were skipping protocol
Partway through the second product, the work was going straight from idea to build. Called a stop with the observation that we were skipping protocol: skipping the user interview and the requirement, and skipping the stack decision. The observation was about the development process itself, not one feature. Contribution: identified that the way we develop the work needed to change. This is the pivotal turn.

## Stage five: training a different way
Asked how to prevent it from recurring. Result: a defined Agent Development Life Cycle with enforced order. Understand the requirement first (structured interview). Choose the stack second, fit to the requirement. Build third, behind a gate. The gate was made real: a build-readiness checklist must be green before a project is ready to build, enforced by a machine check in the verification battery so it cannot be skipped. Contribution: turned a realization into an enforced method.

## Stage six: measuring the quality of knowledge
Set the standard that knowledge quality must be measured, not assumed. Asked whether proven formulas exist to guarantee harvest completeness and note value, like the Wilson formula governs agent capability. Answered in the companion note quality-and-performance-formulas: capture-recapture completeness, agreement statistics, retrieval metrics, calibration, and performance ranking, each with a bar. Contribution: pushed for measurable quality over judgment calls.

## Stage seven: bringing in the architect
Only after product, asset, method, and quality bar were set did the architecture role open. Brought in Nirjhar Das as lead architect, with Shop Online New York as the first engagement, and asked for his input at the end. Contribution: sequenced the hire so the architect starts on solid ground.
