# A plan that staffs two agents and then routes its own review to them is certifying itself through its creations

## Body

The security-arsenal plan designed and staffed Hestia and Bayamanaco in its §6, and set its own
review path as *"AJ, Hestia, Bayamanaco, then the Brain Trust."* Two of the three reviewers were
agents that document brought into existence.

That inverts the ratified `2026-08-06-tyr-never-certifies-itself-aj-plus-second-breaker-do` — which
the same document cited approvingly in the same section. A reviewer created by the thing it reviews
is not independent of it in the way that rule exists to guarantee, and citing the principle while
breaching it is worse than not knowing it.

The mandate `review-depth-reversibility-test` is sharper still: a wrong answer that surfaces only
in production or months later requires a full cold Brain Trust panel **and** a separately cold
reviewer, *neither being the session that wrote the thing*. A security architecture for a
patient-data system is squarely that class.

**In fairness to the outcome:** the panel ran anyway and returned three independent rejections, so
the compromise did not produce a rubber stamp this time. That is luck, not design — the reviewers
had no stake in the document's survival because they had not yet done any work under it. Run the
same panel after those agents have shipped something and the incentive changes.

**The rule:** a reviewer must predate, and be able to outlive, the thing it reviews.
