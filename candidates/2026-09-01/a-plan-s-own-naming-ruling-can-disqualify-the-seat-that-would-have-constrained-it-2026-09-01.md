# A document that renames a reviewer into an agent it charters has removed that reviewer from its own review

## Body

The operator's 2026-09-01 ruling renamed the Brain Trust's **PrivacyDomain** seat to **Hestia**. The
security-arsenal plan then chartered Hestia as one of its two enforcers, and ruled in its §9 — correctly,
under `a-document-must-not-be-reviewed-by-the-agents-it-creates` — that no seat the document creates may
review it: *"not Hestia, not Bayamanaco."*

The consequence, which the document never stated and nobody noticed until the panel sat: **the plan's own
naming ruling disqualified the privacy seat from reviewing the plan.** The docket's defining requirement
was a privacy containment property. The panel lost its privacy lens to the artifact under review.

All five seats rejected the composition 5–0 — a panel unanimously rejecting its own competence to decide.

## The general shape

Two rules that are individually correct compose into a vacancy:

1. A reviewer must not review what it created.
2. Naming is fluid; a seat can be renamed into a role.

Apply (2) and then (1) and the most constraining reviewer disappears, *by the reviewed document's own
correct reasoning*, with no one having decided to remove it.

## The resolution

The conflict attaches to the **enforcement** role, not to the **review lens**. The privacy-and-compliance
lens predates the document; Hestia's charter does not. Seat privacy under a name the document did not
create, recused from anything touching Hestia's own charter.

## The check

Before seating a panel, ask: *did the document under review rename, charter, or absorb any seat on the
roster?* If yes, that seat is conflicted for its new role and must be re-seated under its old lens rather
than left empty. An empty chair is not a neutral outcome — it is a decision made by default, in the
reviewed document's favour.
