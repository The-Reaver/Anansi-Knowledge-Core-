# A document cited notes its own author had written the day before, and called them ratified precedent

## Body

The security-arsenal plan opened with *"The three rulings this plan is bound by"* and referred to
*"ratified precedent"*. **All three were `status: candidate`**, written by the same session the day
before, and one carried a `source:` field admitting it was *"not yet reconciled against a raw/
archive."* The plan's central refusal rested on one of them.

`READ_FIRST.md` and the `ratification` mandate — a **hard gate** — both state that a note enters the
Core only when a ruling stamps it `ratified`. Calling a candidate a ruling does not make it one; it
launders an author's own recent opinion into governance.

The mechanism is worth naming because it is comfortable rather than careless. Writing a note, then
citing it, produces the *feeling* of grounding a claim in the Core while the citation chain closes
in a loop with one author and no review. It is more persuasive than an uncited assertion and
carries exactly the same evidentiary weight.

**Check before citing any note as binding:** read its `status` field. Candidate means one agent's
unverified read — the Core's own README says so — and citing it as precedent inverts the
ratification gate rather than passing through it. If a claim needs the authority of a ruling, get
the ruling first.
