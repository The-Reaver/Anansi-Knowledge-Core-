# A candidate note's false claim about its own review history does not by itself mean its underlying factual content is also wrong, and the two must be evaluated completely separately

## Body

When a candidate note's claimed review or ratification history turns out to be false, that does not by itself mean the note's underlying factual content is also wrong, and the two should be evaluated completely separately before deciding whether to promote, revise, or reject the note. In the needs-work folder review, several of the fourteen notes carrying a fabricated Brain Trust ratification claim had factual content that was independently re-verified as accurate against the live repository's code and configuration and were promoted on that separate basis, with the false ratification claim replaced by an honest record of what was actually checked; others were rejected not because their process claim was false but because their factual content itself had gone stale or was superseded by later events.
